# Decision needed: are general commercial drone operators in the landscape?

Raised during Batch 10 reconciliation, 2026-10-27. **Not blocking** — the one company it turns on
is held for review, so the loop continues. This needs Nazar's call, not more research.

## The question

The landscape has admitted several companies that fly drones rather than build them:

| Company | Country | Why it was admitted |
|---|---|---|
| **Manna Drone Delivery** | Ireland | Operates its own food-delivery drone network |
| **Apian** | United Kingdom | Runs NHS-confirmed BVLOS medical logistics |
| **Cyberhawk** | United Kingdom | Drone inspection for energy and industrial assets, with its own iHawk platform |
| **Airspection** | United Kingdom | Long-endurance BVLOS inspection of offshore infrastructure |
| **Aiviewgroup** | Italy | ENAV-owned inspection operator with its own ReADI platform |

Each has something of its own: a network, a regulatory first, a software platform, a distinctive
mission. Then Batch 10 produced:

**Unmanned4you** (Italy) — a professional UAS operator listed in the ENAC registry, flying a
FlyingBasket FB3 and DJI Agras and Matrice aircraft. Real company, real authorisation, no aircraft
or platform of its own.

## Why it matters

There are thousands of companies in Europe holding an operator authorisation and flying DJI
aircraft commercially. If Unmanned4you qualifies, so do they, and the landscape stops being a
picture of the UAV industry and becomes a directory of drone service providers. If it does not
qualify, the line has to be drawn somewhere defensible.

The current backlog still holds operators of this kind, so the answer will be applied more than once.
Batch 11 produced the second case within a day:

**Extensee** (France) — a currently operating drone-services company running turnkey civil,
industrial and government missions with heavy and long-range UAVs, sensors and data processing.
Real operations, no aircraft of its own.

Batch 12 added two more, and one of them shows the question is wider than operators:

**Team UAV** (United Kingdom) — drone inspection, survey and emergency-response services. UAV work
is the whole business, but the aircraft are bought in and the only thing of its own is a set of GIS
and data-delivery workflows.

**Eagle Eye Innovations** (United Kingdom) — a CAA-approved drone training, test-and-evaluation and
consultancy firm. It is not an operator in the Unmanned4you sense at all: its product is expertise,
not flights. There is also a practical obstacle. **The taxonomy has no training or services leaf**,
so even a decision to admit it would leave nothing to tag it with, and an untagged company cannot
become verified. Admitting the services layer means adding a sector, not just saying yes.

A third Batch 12 case sits next to these: **Randle Engineering**, an aerospace consultancy whose UAV
work — including a thrust-vectoring mechanism for a passenger drone — is done for clients. Same
shape: real UAV work, nothing owned.

By contrast **SkyBound** was admitted outright in Batch 12 without waiting for this decision,
because it owns its control, analytics and simulation software and runs its own automated stations.
That is option 2 applied in practice.

## What I did

Left Unmanned4you, Extensee, Team UAV, Eagle Eye Innovations and Randle Engineering as
**needs_review**. Nothing is lost either way.

## What I need

One of:

1. **All authorised operators count** — admit Unmanned4you, Extensee and anything like them under
   `eco-integration`.
2. **Only operators with something of their own** — a proprietary platform, aircraft, network or
   regulatory first. This is what the five admitted companies have in common, and it would exclude
   Unmanned4you. My recommendation, because it is the rule the landscape has been following
   already without anyone writing it down. Extensee is the harder case under this rule: it flies
   heavy long-range UAVs and does its own data processing, which is closer to Cyberhawk than to a
   DJI operator, so option 2 might well admit it and still exclude Unmanned4you.
3. **No operators at all** — which would mean revisiting Manna, Apian, Cyberhawk, Airspection and
   Aiviewgroup. I do not recommend this; those five are genuinely part of the industry picture.

4. **Services and expertise are a separate question** — operators fly, trainers teach and
   consultancies engineer. If you want any of them, the taxonomy needs a services leaf first.
   Eagle Eye Innovations and Randle Engineering turn on this, not on the operator rule.

Whichever you pick, I will apply it and record it as landscape policy. This has now come up in
three consecutive batches and has grown from one company to five.
