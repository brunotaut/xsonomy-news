# Decision needed: are general commercial drone operators in the landscape?

Raised during Batch 10 reconciliation, 2026-10-27. **Not blocking** — the one company it turns on
is held for review, so the loop continues. This needs Nazar's call, not more research.

## The question

The landscape has admitted several companies that fly drones rather than build them:

| Company | Country | Why it was admitted |
|---|---|---|
| **Manna Drone Delivery** | Ireland | Operates its own food-delivery drone network |
| **Apian** | United Kingdom | Runs NHS-confirmed BVLOS medical logistics |
| **Cyberhawk** | United Kingdom | Drone inspection for energy and industrial assets, with its own iHawk platform |
| **Airspection** | United Kingdom | Long-endurance BVLOS inspection of offshore infrastructure |
| **Aiviewgroup** | Italy | ENAV-owned inspection operator with its own ReADI platform |

Each has something of its own: a network, a regulatory first, a software platform, a distinctive
mission. Then Batch 10 produced:

**Unmanned4you** (Italy) — a professional UAS operator listed in the ENAC registry, flying a
FlyingBasket FB3 and DJI Agras and Matrice aircraft. Real company, real authorisation, no aircraft
or platform of its own.

## Why it matters

There are thousands of companies in Europe holding an operator authorisation and flying DJI
aircraft commercially. If Unmanned4you qualifies, so do they, and the landscape stops being a
picture of the UAV industry and becomes a directory of drone service providers. If it does not
qualify, the line has to be drawn somewhere defensible.

The current backlog still holds operators of this kind, so the answer will be applied more than once.

## What I did

Left Unmanned4you as **needs_review**. Nothing is lost either way.

## What I need

One of:

1. **All authorised operators count** — admit Unmanned4you and anything like it under
   `eco-integration`.
2. **Only operators with something of their own** — a proprietary platform, aircraft, network or
   regulatory first. This is what the five admitted companies have in common, and it would exclude
   Unmanned4you. My recommendation, because it is the rule the landscape has been following
   already without anyone writing it down.
3. **No operators at all** — which would mean revisiting Manna, Apian, Cyberhawk, Airspection and
   Aiviewgroup. I do not recommend this; those five are genuinely part of the industry picture.

Whichever you pick, I will apply it and record it as landscape policy.
