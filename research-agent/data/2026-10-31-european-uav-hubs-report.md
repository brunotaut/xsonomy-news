# European UAV & Counter-UAS Hubs
Country research and map-import handoff · 31 October 2026

## Findings
This first wave contains **61 research records across 32 country reviews**, supported by **110 unique evidence URLs**. **40 records / 43 locality points in 26 countries** meet the minimum map-candidate threshold. They remain research staging records until editorial review.

The catalogue distinguishes test centres, research labs, campuses, incubators, accelerators, programmes and announced developments. A research laboratory is not automatically a regional industrial cluster; a national programme is not automatically a physical test site. The counts describe this catalogue, not the market size.

The most immediately useful interface change is a hub-type filter and a clear distinction between physical test locations, research campuses, delivery/contact cities, and programmes with no pin. Published city markers should be labelled approximate.

## Important distinctions
- C-UAS evidence is explicit for CESA, Systematic/ESCALAD, Cochstedt, the Latvian competence centre and the planned Selija centre, the Greek training centre and the TRTEST countermeasure scope. The evidence strength and operating context differ.
- Unmanned Valley describes current VLOS testing and future BVLOS opportunities in early 2027. These must not become one “BVLOS available” badge.
- The existing Selonia training/testing facility and the announced EU centre at Selija may be related but are not safely interchangeable.
- ATLAS ALPHA and BRAVO are two sites under one operator; AIRlabs is a network with separate test areas; NaviSpot and PSNC share a campus without being identical operators.
- Brave1 and Miltech Sandbox have ecosystem value without a verified single site pin. MITS accelerator is linked to a group already represented in investor data, but it is a separate function.
- Malta Drone Centre’s Attard location is a contact office, not a confirmed flight range. Andenes represents an operator locality, not an offshore flight-test boundary.

## Methodology and limits
- **unit:** Research records include organisations, site records, programmes, candidates and planned developments. This is not a count of independent operational hubs.
- **inclusion:** A documented UAV research, testing, training, acceleration or ecosystem function; broad defence and aviation leads are kept separately when UAV qualification remains unresolved.
- **evidence:** Public primary/operator, government, university or institutional sources. Search excerpts and selected full pages were inspected; not every page was read in full.
- **map_candidate:** Documented relevant function and a public named locality with approximate centroid. This is a minimum research threshold, not completed editorial approval or operational certification.
- **coordinates:** Approximate manually selected place centroids, generally rounded to two decimal places; never runway, flight-volume or precise office geometry.
- **status:** Documented means an official source describes the function. It does not prove current booking availability, commissioning of every planned capability, or open commercial access.
- **recency:** Review date is retrieval date, not date of establishment or confirmation of current services. Older evidence is labelled where it affects eligibility.
- **completeness:** A broad first-wave country review, not exhaustive. NATO test-centre affiliation alone is not proof of UAV/C-UAS scope.
- **language:** All authored public copy is English. Original source titles retain source-language spelling as evidence metadata.
- **publication:** Research staging only. No website, database or publication eligibility has been changed.
